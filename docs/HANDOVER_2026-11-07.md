# WITCHING HOUR // Studio handover

Updated 7 November 2026. Source repository: `ross-mulcahy/FantasyFootballAnalyzer`.

## Current design

WITCHING HOUR uses a 16-bit sports broadcast control room: a locked viewport shell, tactile league switcher, static Satire Wire footer, silver CRT frames, split turf/indigo scorebugs, and a layered Pixel Scott scene. The three Scott assets are calm with mug, RedZone alert pointing at camera, and sweat with microphone. The live desk chooses a pose from the current take. Its white teleprompter has a content-sized bubble; lineup hazards appear only during the RedZone update and are grouped by player. The compact sub-page teleprompter uses the calm portrait.

The Game Day wall name scales with active screen capacity: Quad-Box, Octo-Box, Duodecuple-Box. The spare channel offers an action to connect another league. The league dock remains alphabetical and changes context on the current route. Individual-league Game Day shows all matchups in that league. `ROOTING CONFLICTS` and `REDZONE WIRE` have CRT frames and expandable detail views. The segmented scorebug meter shows **projection share**, not statistical win probability. Missing or partial projections remain clearly marked.

Selected leagues also have full-screen Watch Mode. Auto Director cuts among league matchups, giving fresh provider score changes and relevant NFL plays priority over the standing score. The wire can take a specific story; viewers can hold a channel, advance manually, or resume automatic cuts. Older events retain their verified age. The Sunday Production Desk adds Comeback Watch, an observed-score replay tape, a separate NFL play tape, a local SVG show card, and opt-in original studio cues.

The official highlight feed reads recent metadata from NFL on FOX, NFL on CBS, then NFL. The matcher considers player, play type, date and any stated yardage, but a suggested match is not verified footage. Watch Mode prefers clips marked embeddable, catches actual player errors, tries another official match, and keeps a direct YouTube link when rights block inline viewing. The `YOUTUBE_API_KEY` secret stays in Spacefast Functions; its absence leaves official-channel searches available. YouTube's metadata flag cannot guarantee that a rights claim will allow playback in the site.

## Shared components and page treatments

- `StudioMonitorFrame` owns the physical bezel, recessed scanline screen, channel header, and status LEDs. `CrtMonitorFrame` supplies the same hardware to Game Day.
- `StudioAnchorDesk` and `PixelScott` own the layered Game Day scene and live take logic. `StudioTeleprompterBar` introduces the data views with view-specific commentary.
- `NflTeamChip` replaces bitmap NFL team logos in player rows. `PixelStudioIcon` supplies integer-grid SVG icons without emoji.
- Rosters use lineup and tape-room monitors. Teams Dossier uses franchise cartridges, 10-segment positional bars, and a **10-segment Manager Skill Score** meter. Trades use a Breaking Trade Alert header, central SWAP emblem, and a verdict derived from the existing PAR result. Awards use distinct trophy, crown, horseshoe, dumpster, and activity pixel icons.
- Other principal data routes, including Draft Grades, Waivers, Luck & PAR, Players, History, Recap, and Settings, mount the studio primitives. Analytical outliers use green/red tinting without changing calculations.

## Route audit

The active routes in `src/App.tsx` were compared with their page components. Game Day uses its larger anchor desk in place of the compact bar. The landing and utility routes use their own framed cards. Player headshots remain in scouting views; those are player portraits, not NFL team logos. No Unicode emoji or `#FFFFFF` borders were found in non-test `src/pages` or `src/components` source. Some CSS modules still carry older declarations followed by later studio overrides, and some analytical sub-panels nest a CRT inside another CRT; these are maintainability cleanup candidates, not blocking visual gaps.

## Sunday production desk

The Hub now adds a recent-event Director's Cue that focuses the relevant monitor without changing league order. Studio 02 below the wall has four views: Comeback Watch, Instant Replay, Halftime / Final, and Studio Audio. Comeback Watch uses current provider scores and known remaining starters. Replay stores at most 150 score-change frames in this browser and presents NFL play history separately; no exact fantasy point delta is attributed to a play. The show card exports a local SVG built from current results, the largest recorded score swing, a same-position bench comparison, and the biggest rooting paradox. New synthesized lead-change and major-swing sounds are individually opt-in and respect the existing global mute. See `docs/GAME_DAY_GUIDE.md` for user-facing limitations and controls.
